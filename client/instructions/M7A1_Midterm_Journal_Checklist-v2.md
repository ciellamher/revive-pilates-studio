# 📓 M7A1: Midterm Reflection Journal - Master Instructions & Checklist (No-Code Version)
**Grade Value:** 25 Points  
**Submission Requirement:** A direct GitHub link to your completed `project/04-reflection-journal.md` file.

---

## 🎯 Core Objective
Conduct a retrospective reflection on your learning journey across Javascript, React, CSS/Styling, Node/Express, REST APIs, and PostgreSQL databases. 

⚠️ **The Two Golden Rules for Grading:**
1.  **Honesty Costs Zero Points:** Do not pretend you understand everything perfectly. Acknowledging your struggles and detailing what is shaky is highly rewarded on this rubric.
2.  **Be Tech-Specific:** Avoid vague phrasing (for example, saying "databases were hard" is poor). Use precise terms and mention exact error messages or bugs (such as "understanding why my React frontend form could not access my Express backend due to Cross-Origin Resource Sharing policy blocking" is excellent).

---

## 📌 Step-by-Step Template Prompts
Your journal file must answer the following questions in complete, thoughtful sentences. Do not use code blocks for your descriptions; write everything in standard prose:

### 💡 Part A: The Frontend in Your Own Words
1.  **JavaScript Fundamentals:** Detail the core fundamentals (variables, functions, arrays, objects). What specific concept finally made complete sense to you, and what clicked it into place?
2.  **React Architecture:** Imagine explaining to a student in the cohort below what a React component actually is. How would you explain why component state is fundamentally different from a standard JavaScript local variable?
3.  **Styling & Design:** What did you learn about making a user interface look deliberate, custom, and professional rather than relying on default browser styling?

### 💡 Part B: The Backend Half
1.  **Node and Express:** What is a server actually doing when it listens? What exactly is a backend route?
2.  **RESTful API Design:** In your own words, what makes an API endpoint RESTful, and what do the standard HTTP verbs (GET, POST, PUT, DELETE) represent? Name one route you wrote yourself in class and the precise data object type it returned.
3.  **PostgreSQL Databases:** What does a relational database (SQL) give you that a standard JavaScript array in-memory cannot? Detail one query you were proud of writing, or one database query that took you over an hour to debug.
4.  **Connecting the Stack:** What surprised you the most about getting your React frontend forms to successfully communicate with your custom Express API backend?

### 💡 Part C: What Clicked, and What Is Still Shaky
*Use precise examples naming your actual lab activities and assignments.*
1.  **The Breakthrough:** Describe one specific technical concept that finally clicked for you, and what resource, video, or debugging process made it make sense.
2.  **The Confusion:** What is one specific technical concept you still find highly confusing, and where exactly do you get stuck?
3.  **The Pride Moment:** Describe one bug or complex coding problem you successfully solved yourself that you are incredibly proud of. What was the bug, how did you track it down, and what was the root fix?
4.  **Recognizable Error:** Name one specific error message you now recognize on sight and explain what it usually means in plain English (such as a Failed to Fetch error, a Cannot Read Properties of Undefined error, or a Four-Zero-Four Not Found error).

### 💡 Part D: How I Work
1.  **Debugging Strategies:** When you hit a major roadblock now, what is your first course of action? Has this process changed since the first day of class?
2.  **AI Tools Policy:** Be completely honest about how you use generative AI assistants. Mention where they have handed you code that you could not explain, or code that completely broke.
3.  **Procrastination Lesson:** Which specific course activity or lab did you leave to the very last minute, and what did that procrastination cost you?
4.  **Habit Review:** What is one positive development habit you will keep for the final project build, and what is one bad habit you are committed to changing?

### 💡 Part E: Looking Forward to the Final Project
1.  **Stack Core:** Which specific module or lab activity from the course does your final project proposal lean on the most? (Point directly to a specific route or section in your plan).
2.  **The Technical Gap:** What is the biggest gap between what you are capable of building today and what your proposal plan demands?
3.  **Two-Week Checklist:** What is one concrete, verifiable task you will have completed exactly two weeks from today? (Do not write "started coding"—list a checkable milestone like "database tables set up and Express API routes successfully returning mock JSON data").

## 📊 M7A1 Reflection Journal Grading Rubric (25 Points)

*   **Coverage of the Course (7 pts):**
    *   *Excellent (6-7 pts):* Thoughtfully covers JS, React, styling, backend servers, and database topics in the student's own voice.
    *   *Satisfactory (4-5 pts):* Speaks to some of the modules, leaving a few blank or thin.
    *   *Needs Work (0-3 pts):* Highly generic summaries or skips most course topics.
*   **Honesty & Specificity (8 pts):**
    *   *Excellent (7-8 pts):* Provides concrete, detailed examples of what clicked, what remains shaky, and specific bugs/errors solved.
    *   *Satisfactory (5-6 pts):* Some specific examples, but diluted with general filler content.
    *   *Needs Work (0-4 pts):* Vague entries that could describe any student in the class.
*   **Connection to the Project (7 pts):**
    *   *Excellent (6-7 pts):* Directly links course learnings and technical gaps to the final project proposal documents.
    *   *Satisfactory (4-5 pts):* Mentions the final project briefly, with a loose connection to planning.
    *   *Needs Work (0-3 pts):* No link made between learnings and the upcoming build.
*   **Effort & Length (3 pts):**
    *   *Excellent (3 pts):* Highly thoughtful reflection within the 500 to 700 word range.
    *   *Satisfactory (2 pts):* Present but thin, containing minimal reflection.
    *   *Needs Work (0-1 pts):* Rushed, extremely short, or only a few lines long.
